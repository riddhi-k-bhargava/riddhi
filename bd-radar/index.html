Why it needed to exist

Getting to a deal early is the whole game.

A JV advisory firm wins by getting to a deal early. Once an announcement is public, corp dev and legal are fielding calls within days, and the first credible outreach usually wins the work. Eight people were scanning the news by hand: slow, patchy, and impossible to measure. I wanted to turn that into something the firm owns and that gets sharper over time, so outreach goes out faster and we can finally see our own coverage.

Strategy

Agent systems break at the seams, not on model quality.

They break where state moves between steps and where autonomy should stop. So I built around two things: a single source of truth every agent shares, the system's memory, and a clear line between what the system decides and what a person signs off on. Get those right and the rest is plumbing.

What I did

The calls I made.

01
Split agents by job. Writers write, formatters talk, sending happens in flows. More moving parts, but the flood-the-team failure is gone by design.
02
Made the shared key immutable and told it to fail loud. If the read it depends on breaks, the system stops instead of guessing. A quietly corrupted intelligence file is worse than a late one.
03
Drew the autonomy line at anything a client sees or any record that becomes truth. Emails are drafted, never sent. Writes need a confirm.
04
Matched coverage on the key, never on similar names. A false "we've got this" hides a real gap, which costs more than a visible hole.
05
Fixed the actual bottleneck, a shared API rate limit and silent date corruption, not the model. Put the work into scheduling, pacing, and date handling.

What I built. The whole thing. Nine agents plus scheduled flows over a shared data layer, live and running on its own every day for the team.

What it shows. The judgment agent products turn on: where autonomy should stop, and how state survives the handoffs between agents. Backed by real guardrail and eval habits rather than a demo.

Architecture

Nine agents, one shared state layer.

Architecture summary. A nightly chain of agents runs two hours apart. Manzama ingests watchlist news at 1 AM. Agent 1A classifies a deal at 3 AM and writes to the JV_DealLog table, creating an immutable DealID. Agent 2 finds contacts at 5 AM and writes to Daily_Targets. Agent 1B reads JV_DealLog and Daily_Targets at 7 AM and drafts a digest, writing nothing. A person approves. The digest is saved to Outlook drafts at 9 AM and staging is cleared. All four tables share one immutable DealID.

Human approval required

The system has no send capability. It has classified the deal, found contacts, and drafted the digest, and it has staged everything. Approve to write the digest into Outlook drafts. You send it, not the system.

Results

Live, and measured.

Running unattended. Live every day for the 8-person team, on its own.

The loop, fixed by design. 15 duplicate outputs per run down to 1, with no more duplicate writes or broken IDs.

Rate limits solved by redesign, not spend. I traced the throttling to the platform underneath the vendor, so a pricier tier would not have touched it. The fix was scheduling and pacing.

Coverage. Roughly 2,000 companies watched, 2,238 contacts managed, 95+ deals classified and growing.

Time saved, estimated. Cut about 13 hours a week of manual deal monitoring. Method: 8 people at about 20 minutes each a day, 5 days a week. The 20-minute figure is the input to adjust; the agent count, watchlist, contacts, and deal counts are measured.
