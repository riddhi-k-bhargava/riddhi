The opportunity

The registry is a moment. The deciding is months.

Babylist sees what parents add and what guests buy. It sees almost none of the weeks of comparing, and none of the trusted opinions that actually drive the choice, because that work happens off-platform. The Consideration List brings that phase home, and turns a one-time event into an ongoing relationship.

What's in it for the parents

Your whole baby-prep decision, in one place.

The parent promise is simple: run the entire deciding process, start to finish, inside Babylist, and collaborate with the people you would otherwise be texting.

01
Start and finish in one place. Every option you're weighing, from stroller to bottles, lives in one private list instead of scattered across tabs, screenshots, and group chats.
02
The trusted second opinion, built in. Invite the few people whose judgment you trust and get their input exactly where you're stuck, without leaving the app or chasing replies.
03
Buy what you actually need. Honest input, including "you don't need this," means less overwhelm, fewer returns, and a registry you feel genuinely confident about.
04
Commit on your terms. Once you've decided, add it straight to your registry: visible for guests to gift, or hidden if it's something you plan to buy yourselves.
What's in it for Babylist

Higher-intent revenue, a data moat, and lock-in by love.

01
Higher-converting, higher-gifted adds. Considered commitments convert and get purchased by guests at higher rates than impulse adds, and get returned less.
02
A net-new, high-margin data product. The rejection trail, what got considered, cut, and which competitor it lost to, is first-party signal brands will pay The Push to see. Sold as labeled presence and intent signal, never as a guaranteed win.
03
Lock-in by love. It captures the off-platform graph, accumulates personal investment the parent won't want to abandon, and extends the relationship into the multi-year arc Babylist already bets on with milestone lists and Babylist Money.

What could break it

Two real risks, with the guardrails that hold them.

Risk 01 · Cannibalization

Deciding delays committing.

If parking items to consider becomes the easy default, it could pull parents away from actually committing them to the registry, the step that drives gifting and revenue.

Mitigation. Keep committing to the registry the primary, most prominent path, and gate launch on hard guardrails: registry-add rate and gifting GMV must not fall. Prove it with a click test before any rollout.
Risk 02 · Trust erosion

Monetizing advice corrodes the brand.

If Babylist ever tilted the suggestions or the collaborator input parents see toward paid brands, it would undercut the unbiased guidance that is its most valuable asset.

Mitigation. A strict trust wall: monetize the intent signal and labeled presence, never the outcome. Ship "you don't need it" prominently, and run one test every release: would the set look the same if Babylist sold none of these products?
Validate first. The cannibalization guardrail and a 30-family concierge test of the collaborative phase answer the two make-or-break questions before any build. See it in action: rb-mocks-babylist-collaboration.lovable.app
