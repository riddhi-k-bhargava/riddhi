How it decides

Timing is anchored to the CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early." milestone checklists (2022 revision, the 75th percentile ages at which most babies show a skill) and the WHO windows for the six gross motor milestones. Those set when a piece of gear tends to become useful or outgrown.

It does not blindly trust the "using" switch. If your baby has likely aged past an item's useful window, it moves to pack away with the typical age noted. For safety-adjacent items like car seat inserts and swaddles, it points you to the product's own weight and height limits and current safe-sleep guidance rather than a hard age.

The on-track line below any result is a non-diagnostic reflection of what you entered against those public references. It is not a developmental screen and it cannot see your child. If you have any concern about development, talk to your pediatrician and ask about a formal screening.

The bias is toward owning less. Borrowing, buying secondhand, and waiting are treated as valid answers, and "you do not need this yet" is a real recommendation, not a filler.

Not medical advice. This is a working prototype for organizing baby gear, not a medical or developmental tool. Milestone references are public population averages and every baby is different. Nothing here diagnoses or screens for anything. Practice ideas like propping to sit are for supervised, awake time only, never for sleep and never unattended. For questions about your child's health, development, feeding, or safe sleep, contact a qualified clinician.
