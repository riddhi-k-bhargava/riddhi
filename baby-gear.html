The problem

New parents drown in gear.

Gifts, hand-me-downs, and well-meaning registries pile up, and almost none of it answers the only question that matters: is this right for my baby, right now? A bassinet is useful for three months and then becomes a hazard. An infant car seat insert protects a newborn and then works against an older baby.

Registries are built to sell more, not to help you use what fits. The result is clutter, wasted money, and a low background worry about whether you have the "right" things.

Vision & strategy

A tracker that reasons about stage, not a list that just holds things.

Vision. A tracker that reasons about your baby's stage instead of just holding a list. You tell it what you own and what your baby can do. It tells you what to use now, what to prepare for next month, and what you have already outgrown, always favoring using what you have over buying something new.

Strategy. Multimodal models are now cheap and accurate enough to classify a photographed item for a fraction of a cent, and capable enough to reason about stage-appropriateness against real milestone data. The differentiation is not "AI identifies an object." It is a constrained classification taxonomy wired into a stage-aware, minimalist needs engine, with cost, evals, and privacy treated as first-class. This is the same catalog-plus-ML classification pattern from my marketplace work, pointed at a parent's shelf instead of a seller's listing.

What the demo does

What it actually does.

01
Point and log. Photograph any item, and the model classifies its category, product type, and size band. You confirm before it enters your catalog.
02
Stage-aware sorting. Every item lands in one of five buckets, from get-now to coming-next-month to already-outgrown.
03
Outgrown nudges. Even when you mark something as "using," it flags items your baby has likely aged past, such as a bassinet after three to four months.
04
Goal-aware planning. Set a goal for the week or month, like start solids or sit independently, and it suggests the minimum you need, preferring what you already own.
05
Milestone-anchored. Readiness reasoning follows CDC (2022, 75th percentile) and WHO milestones, and it never diagnoses.

Architecture. A static frontend on GitHub Pages with no build step, a Cloudflare Worker as the backend API (deployed from GitHub, with KV for storage and a weekly cron trigger), and Google Gemini Flash-Lite for the two AI steps. The browser downscales a photo and posts it to the Worker's /scan endpoint; the Worker asks Gemini to classify it into a fixed taxonomy and returns a suggestion to confirm. Profile and inventory persist in KV. A /recommend call sends profile and inventory to Gemini and gets back the five buckets plus goal suggestions. A weekly cron re-runs the recommendation so the plan stays current.

Where AI is used, and where it is not. The model does the fuzzy judgment, recognizing an item from a photo and reasoning about stage-appropriateness and minimal purchases. Deterministic code does everything that must be exact: storage, the age calculation, the taxonomy, and the response shape. Numbers are never generated by the model. Guardrails: outputs are constrained to a fixed JSON shape and a fixed category list, low-confidence classifications ask instead of guessing, and photos are classified then discarded, never stored. A scan runs about a twentieth of a cent on a Flash-Lite model, which is where the modeled cost of a couple of cents per user per month comes from.

General guidance, not medical advice. Follow your pediatrician and current safe-sleep guidance. Milestone references are public population averages and every baby is different, so nothing here diagnoses or screens for anything.
